# Illustration brief — "The tale of Fatt & Pundit"

For the illustrator. One comic, 13 panels, told on the Fatt Pundit website as you scroll. The full story lives at `/our-story/`; the five panels marked ★ also appear as a short version on the home page.

**Client sign-off first:** Fatt and Pundit are characters built from the restaurant's name, as the restaurant itself explains it: "Fatt", a common Chinese surname, and "Pundit", the Indian word for a scholar. They are an allegory of how Indo-Chinese food began, not portraits of the founders. The client approves the characters and this script before drawing starts.

**Start from what exists:** the Soho room opened in 2019 with "paintings of the 'Fatt Pundit' himself" on the walls (restaurantonline.co.uk, 1 March 2019). Ask the client for photos of those paintings and the Tangra-style murals: the characters should feel like they come from the same hand.

## The story in one line

A Hakka cook leaves Canton for Kolkata, meets a scholar who lives to eat, and together they turn Chinese technique and Indian spice into a new cuisine that later travels to London and Dubai. (Plot from the brand guidelines, menu back, p.27.)

## Characters

- **Fatt.** Young Hakka cook from Canton. Practical, quick, warm, never without his wok. Plain cook's clothes of the period, sleeves rolled. His wok and cleaver are props he carries through the whole story.
- **Pundit.** Kolkata scholar who lives to eat. Round spectacles, a book under his arm, a shawl or kurta. Opinionated about flavour and always right about chilli.
- Draw both as real, likeable people. **No caricature or ethnic stereotyping**: no exaggerated features, no costume clichés, no "funny accent" body language. Their friendship is the heart of the story; let them argue and laugh like friends.
- Keep faces, proportions and clothes consistent across all 13 panels (a character sheet first, please: front, side, three expressions each).

## Look

- **Vintage, warm, printed.** Think old travel posters and Indian matchbox labels: flat colour, visible texture, slightly misregistered print, hand-drawn line.
- **Palette: brand colours only** (guidelines p.17): ink `#2C2925`, tiger `#F15D3D`, gold `#CBA052`, sand `#DAC7B5`, slate `#758591`, plus tints of these. No other hues.
- Brand art can appear inside scenes: the tiger, the mountains and the ship from the guidelines (brand assets, p.22) suit Tangra walls, ship sails and shop signs.
- **No logos** inside the art (the logo is never rotated, cropped or placed on busy pictures: guidelines p.16).
- **No words drawn in the art**: captions and speech bubbles are live text on the page (readable on phones, translatable, searchable). Shop signs can carry made-up letterforms, not readable words.
- Leave the area named in each panel's note quiet (sky, wall, steam) so the speech bubble sits on calm ground.

## Delivery

- Wide panels: 2520 × 1080 px (21:9). Half panels: 1600 × 1200 px (4:3). Keep important action out of the outer 6% (it can crop on phones).
- Flat PNG or high-quality JPG, sRGB. Name each file after its panel id, e.g. `p05-first-taste.jpg`.
- Drop them into `src/assets/images/story/`. The site picks each one up and replaces that panel's storyboard sketch; nothing else changes. The site makes the web-sized copies itself.
- Please also send the layered source files (PSD/Procreate) for future edits.
